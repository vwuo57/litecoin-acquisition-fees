## The four real ways to buy LTC

Each one trades off cost against control.

**A centralized exchange (CEX).** Register, verify identity, fund the account, buy. This is the route most people want, because you can use a bank transfer or card, and the order book gives you a transparent price. The tradeoff: the exchange holds your coins until you withdraw them.

**A self-custody wallet with a built-in fiat ramp.** You keep the keys, but you're paying a third-party on-ramp, and those are usually the priciest card fees in the market.

**A decentralized exchange.** No registration, no KYC, but LTC isn't a native smart-contract asset, so you're typically buying a wrapped version — and wrapped LTC is not the same thing as LTC. Watch the contract address, not the ticker.

**P2P.** You trade directly with another person, the platform escrows the funds, and the platform itself often charges no fee on the trade. You're comparing seller quotes instead, which is the actual cost here.

For most people buying LTC for the first time, the CEX path is the one that costs the least and explains itself most clearly.

**Spread versus fee.** Convert is the fastest way to buy LTC, and it's not free — the cost is baked into the exchange rate. On a small buy that's convenient. On a large one, compare it against a limit order on the LTC/USDT book before you click.

**Network choice on withdrawal.** If you're moving LTC off the exchange to your own wallet, the network you select has to match the receiving wallet's. This is the single most common way people lose coins, and it is not reversible. Send a small test amount first.

**Where you keep it afterwards.** Exchange custody is convenient and lets you sell in seconds. Self-custody means the keys are yours, along with the responsibility.
